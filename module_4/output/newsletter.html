This Week in Music

Explore this week’s new releases, emerging voices and tour news across Rock, Latin, Metal, and Alternative & Punk.

Rock

Friday’s release slate leans heavy, with a few different shades of rock in the mix. Southern rock veterans Molly Hatchet return with Lost Horizon, while King 810’s La Grande Mort Or Silence Of God and Leviathan’s Die To This bring a darker edge. Metal fans also have Devastator’s Goathammer EP and a new album, In Metal, from Mystic Prophecy to check out.

For a more expansive, melodic listen, Neal Morse & The Resonance release River Of Light, adding a progressive-rock counterpoint to the week’s tougher sounds. Taken together, the releases make for a Friday built around full-length statements and riff-forward intensity—with the Devastator EP offering a shorter blast. Whether your playlist leans toward Southern-rock grit, extreme-metal weight or prog’s bigger canvas, this week’s new arrivals give rock listeners several distinct routes to explore.

Latin

Latin music’s newest wave is moving in several directions at once. The Latin Recording Academy’s Next Up spotlight pairs emerging voices like Puerto Rican artist Matt Louis—whose Afro-Caribbean palette draws on reggaetón, dembow, bolero and soul—with Ecuadorian singer Jombriel, a reggaetón and dancehall artist whose “Vitamina,” with DFZM and JØTTA, reached Spotify’s Global charts. Mariangela is also extending her emotionally candid run with the new single “En El Fondo,” following “La 17” and “Cuando Una Mujer.”

For a more left-field listen, Argentinian duo Peces Raros have dropped “Conexiones,” the first preview of their forthcoming sixth album. The track opens a new chapter for Lucio Consolo and Marco Viera, whose alternative-electronic approach pushes beyond familiar Latin-pop formulas. Together, the releases show a scene where rhythmic roots remain central, but artists are stretching the sound—from intimate, personal songwriting to club-minded experimentation.

Metal

Metal’s new-release slate came in heavy this week: Trivium’s Crown in the Grave and Suicide Silence’s Graceless arrived alongside Down’s Volume V—the New Orleans sludge veterans’ first studio album since 2007. Down also previewed the record with “They Walk The Night.” Elsewhere, the week’s releases ranged from Azarath’s Scorn Eternal to Disfear’s The Last Nail and Domkraft’s Night Songs, a broad spread of extreme metal, crust and doom.

There’s more on the horizon, too. Bruce Dickinson’s “Speaking in Tongues” and Kerry King’s “My War” are among this week’s new tracks, with solo albums PsychoGod and The End Of All Gods announced for March 12 and February 12, respectively. For something more atmospheric, Birmingham psych-doomers Meatdripper released their debut EP, Into The Golden Orb. Fresh albums now, big-name solo projects next: metal’s calendar is staying busy.

Alternative & Punk

This week’s guitar-led release slate spans shadowy post-punk and scrappy New Orleans rock. London’s Desperate Journalist return with Gilding the Lily, while New Orleans duo Twisted Teens bring their Sub Pop debut, The Holy Cross Tigers—their third album of the year, a notably prolific run for a band still carving out its space. Both records arrive November 20, offering two different routes through the alternative underground.

The punk calendar is busy beyond Friday’s new albums: Toronto veterans Fucked Up released Year of the Rooster last week, the final chapter in their Grass Can Move Stones trilogy. And Fall Out Boy have put fresh pop-punk momentum on the horizon, announcing a 2027 North American tour with The Offspring, Good Charlotte and The Story So Far, alongside new single “ACES.” The schedule promises a generational handoff, with arena-sized hooks meeting a new wave of releases from independent rock bands.
